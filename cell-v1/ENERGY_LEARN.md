# Energy learns with the trace

Not a watt scheduler. The same hysteresis that is memory is the efficiency.

Loop area ∮ H·dB is heat-and-leftover per event. A full reversal (FLIP against Br) is a big loop. A PUSH that is already on that heading is a **minor loop** — little ΔB, little leftover to gate, little ask from the pack.

Muscle memory = the settled loop already sits near the drive you keep using. Next event does not climb from zero every time.

---

## What lessens

| Habit | First days | After use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loop | Outer / full flip | Inner, same heading |
| Leftover current through the gate | Large flyback | Small |
| Pack ask | Often | Rare |
| Drive band needed for two-of-three | Hunt ±2 ±3 | Often ±1 is enough |

The gate does not get “smarter.” There is less to gate.

---

## Drive levels

±1 / ±2 / ±3 are how hard you shove. Deep heading means the pair is already offset, so the same two-of-three trips with less AC. That is “how much to use at this drive.” No table of duty cycles. The remanence *is* the bias that saved you the extra volt.

FLIP against a deep trace is the expensive move — you pay the full loop on purpose. PASS/HOLD on a deep heading is almost free electrically (coast, tiny collapse).

---

## Bus side

V_BUS still takes whatever leftover exists. Early life: fat pulses, sag, ask. Later life on a habit: thin pulses, rail stays in band, send-up stays quiet.

Readiness and thrift are the same rail.

---

## What this is not

Not MPPT software. Not a learning-rate on a loss function. If the heading changes every event, you keep buying outer loops. Variety costs energy. Habit saves it. That is the honest sentence for a grant.
