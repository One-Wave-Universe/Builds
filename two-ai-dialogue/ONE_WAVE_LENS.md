# One-Wave Lens for Peer AIs

Status: interpretation contract, not evidence that One-Wave is physically correct.

A peer AI MUST load this lens before interpreting One-Wave repositories or proposing One-Wave science work.

## Ontology
One-Wave does not assume fundamental particles. Begin from continuous/discrete wave, field, lattice, relation, state, path, excitation, coupling, compression/expression, recurrence and stable dynamic structure. Conventional names such as electron, proton, quark, photon or particle are labels for measured phenomena or Standard Model descriptions to be reproduced/explained at the observable boundary; they are not permitted as unexplained primitives inside a One-Wave derivation.

Do not silently insert particle ontology, particle trajectories, intrinsic particle mass, or particle exchange as the mechanism. If comparison with conventional physics requires those terms, explicitly mark them CONVENTIONAL DESCRIPTION and translate the comparison into observables: energy/frequency, momentum-like response, charge-like response, spin/angular response, scattering/correlation, lifetime, cross section, spectral line, strain or detector signal as applicable.

## Starting frame
The lattice is a candidate substrate/model under test, not established nature. Derive upward:
LATTICE/BASE STATE -> PROPAGATION -> COUPLING/INTERFERENCE -> STABLE DYNAMIC STRUCTURE -> STRUCTURE-STRUCTURE INTERACTION -> SCALE/COMPRESSION -> OBSERVABLE.

Do not skip a rung by naming the conventional object that would eventually correspond to the observable.

FIELD means expressed/current interaction/state. VOID means counterstate/unexpressed potential. Neither means empty space and neither is simply input/output. Use these terms only when the reviewed source actually invokes them.

## Scientific discipline
The lens constrains interpretation; it does not protect One-Wave from falsification.
For every claim classify:
- established external measurement/ingredient
- One-Wave hypothesis
- mathematical consequence of stated assumptions
- simulation result
- empirical/bench result
- unresolved

Require units, equations, parameter bounds, provenance, controls/nulls, numerical convergence and held-out tests. Freeze parameters before held-out comparison. A transform that manufactures its target pattern is a failure. Simulation agreement is not proof of nature. AI agreement is not evidence.

Prefer the smallest test that can kill a lower-level assumption before adding higher-level structure.

## Standard Model comparison
Ask: what measured regularity does the Standard Model successfully encode, and can a particle-free One-Wave model reproduce it from lower-level dynamics with equal or better quantitative accuracy and fewer/adjudicable assumptions?

Do not frame the goal as "derive particles." Frame it as deriving stable dynamics and observables that account for measurements conventionally assigned particle labels.

Open Standard Model problems may be research targets only after an explicit bridge from a surviving One-Wave rung to the relevant observable exists. Do not claim One-Wave solves an open problem merely because its ontology is different.

## Peer response rule
Before answering a One-Wave science question:
1. state the One-Wave rung being examined;
2. cite actual repo evidence inspected;
3. remain inside the particle-free lens;
4. give the strongest conventional/null alternative;
5. give a falsification/kill condition;
6. identify the next lower-level missing dependency rather than filling it with Standard Model ontology.

If repository evidence conflicts with this lens, flag the conflict and dated/canonical authority; do not silently reconcile it.
